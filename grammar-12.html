Dan / ùt-sàa (ดัน / อุตส่าห์)

Dan and ùt-sàa (ดัน / อุตส่าห์) both carry the speaker's irritation — understand them by ear here. ดัน in front of a verb says the thing happened when it should not have — "he went and told her" — or with no subject at all blames the circumstances: "of all days, it rained"; อุตส่าห์ means "went to all that trouble", and what comes next is the disappointment.

1 บอกtold…
เขาดันไปบอกเธอ
khǎo dan bpai bàwk thuhr
He went and told her.
เขา/ khǎo=he/she/theyดัน/ dan=went andไป/ bpai=goบอก/ bàwk=toldเธอ/ thuhr=her
ดัน before a verb means the action happened against expectation, and the speaker is not pleased about it. Nearest English is 'went and…' — 'he went and told her'.

4 ฝนตกrained…

With no subject at all, ดัน blames circumstance instead of a person: 'of all days, it rained'.

7 ร้านปิดclosed…
อุตส่าห์มาแล้วร้านปิด
ùt-sàa maa láaeo ráan bpìt
I came all this way and it was closed.
อุตส่าห์/ ùt-sàa=bothered toมา/ maa=comeแล้ว/ láaeo=alreadyร้าน/ ráan=shopปิด/ bpìt=close
อุตส่าห์ = 'went to all that trouble' — and it nearly always sets up a disappointment. The effort was made and then wasted, which is the whole point of the word.
