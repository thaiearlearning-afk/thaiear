Long vowels

Thai vowels attach to a consonant — above, below, before, after, or wrapped right around it. The ◌ in each symbol shows where the consonant sits. Note that some vowels are written BEFORE the consonant but spoken after it: เ◌, แ◌, โ◌. These 9 are the long vowels — held noticeably longer, roughly twice the length of their short partners. Length matters: it changes meaning, and (as you'll see in the tone rules) it can change the tone.

Vowel names all start with sara (สระ — “vowel”). Tap a card to hear the name, or the pill to hear a real word that uses it.
◌า
sara aa
aa — as in "father"
◌ี
sara ii
ii — as in "see"
◌ือ
sara uue
uue — "oo" with unrounded lips
◌ู
sara uu
uu — as in "moon"
เ◌
sara ee
ee — as in "pay" (no y-glide)
แ◌
sara aae
aae — as in "fair"
โ◌
sara oo
oo — as in "go" (no w-glide)
◌อ
sara aw
aw — as in "saw"
เ◌อ
sara uhr
uhr — as in "eugh" (the sound of disgust)
