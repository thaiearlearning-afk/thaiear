Nàwy / làawk (หน่อย / หรอก)

Nàwy and làawk (หน่อย / หรอก) both soften, in opposite directions — understand them by ear here. หน่อย means "a little" and turns an order into a request; หรอก takes the edge off a "no" — "really, it's fine" rather than a flat refusal, and leaving it out makes the same sentence noticeably blunter.

1 ช่วยhelp…
ช่วยหน่อยได้ไหมคะ
chûai nòi dâi mǎi khá
Could you help me?
ช่วย/ chûai=helpหน่อย/ nòi=(please)ได้/ dâi=canไหม/ mǎi=(question)คะ/ khá=polite(f)
หน่อย = 'a little', and it softens a request into something politely small. Adding it is most of what makes an imperative sound courteous rather than blunt.

4 ไม่เป็นไรnothing…
ไม่เป็นไรหรอกครับ

หรอก softens a contradiction or a refusal. It says 'really, it's fine' rather than a flat no — dropping it makes the same sentence noticeably blunter.
