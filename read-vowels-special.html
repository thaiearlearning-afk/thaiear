Special vowels

The leftovers — and some of the most common syllables in Thai. Four of them secretly contain their own ending sound: ◌ำ ends in m, ใ◌ and ไ◌ end in y, เ◌า ends in w — so syllables built on them count as 'livei' for the tone rules. ใ◌ and ไ◌ sound identical; only 20 words use ใ◌ (all others use ไ◌). The three gliding vowels (เ◌ีย, เ◌ือ, ◌ัว) slide from one vowel into another. ฤ is a rare consonant-vowel hybrid you'll mostly meet in ฤดู (season).

Vowel names all start with sara (สระ — “vowel”). Tap a card to hear the name, or the pill to hear a real word that uses it.
◌ำ
sara am
am — as in "scramble" (ends in m → live)
ใ◌
sara ai (mái múan)
ai — as in "eye" (only 20 words use it)
ไ◌
sara ai (mái má-laai)
ai — as in "eye" (the usual spelling)
เ◌า
sara ao
ao — as in "cow" (ends in w → live)
◌ัว
sara ua
ua — glides "oo → ah"
เ◌ีย
sara ia
ia — glides "ee → ah"
เ◌ือ
sara uea
uea — glides "uue → ah"
ฤ
rue
rúe / rí — a consonant-vowel hybrid
